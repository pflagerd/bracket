If you're raising money in the U.S., manufacturing in the U.S., and selling in both the U.S. and Scandinavia, then you are no longer building a regional company—you are building an international hardware brand from day one.

That gives you several advantages.

Why this strategy makes sense

United States

  • Kickstarter has one of the world's largest crowdfunding audiences.

  • "Made in USA" is a strong selling point for many buyers.

  • Shipping within the U.S. is simpler and less expensive than international fulfillment.

  • A successful Kickstarter creates proof of demand that distributors respect.

Scandinavia

  • You already understand the market and culture.

  • Scandinavian consumers appreciate functional, minimalist, high-quality products.

  • Your existing network can help you secure early retail and distributor relationships.

I would build the company in three phases

Phase 1 – Kickstarter (United States)

The goal isn't just to raise money.

It's to prove there is a market.

A successful campaign gives you:

  • Thousands of customers

  • Product reviews

  • Professional photography and videos

  • Press coverage

  • Revenue

  • Credibility

  • Data on which products and price points perform best

That makes conversations with distributors much easier.


Phase 2 – Build Distribution

Instead of trying to sell to thousands of stores individually, I would focus on finding master distributors.

For example:

United States

  • Home improvement distributors

  • Industrial hardware distributors

  • Storage and organization distributors

  • Specialty woodworking suppliers

  • Commercial shelving suppliers

Scandinavia

  • DIY chains

  • Building supply distributors

  • Home organization distributors

  • Furniture and interior design wholesalers

One distributor can often provide access to hundreds or even thousands of retail locations.

Phase 3 – Direct-to-Consumer

Continue selling through your own website.

This gives you:

  • Higher margins

  • Customer data

  • Repeat purchases

  • Email marketing opportunities

  • A platform for future product launches

The strongest hardware brands typically combine:

  • Direct online sales

  • Distributor sales

  • Retail partnerships

rather than relying on just one channel.

I would also add one more objective

If I were presenting this to investors or writing a business plan, I wouldn't describe the company as:

"We manufacture shelf brackets."

I'd describe it as:

"We are building a premium modular storage and mounting systems company focused on innovative, high-strength hardware for residential and commercial spaces."

That positioning gives you room to expand into complementary products over time.

My strategic recommendation

Given your background in international sales and business development, I would treat the Kickstarter campaign as more than a way to fund manufacturing. I'd use it to build a portfolio of evidence:

  • Sales traction: Number of backers and total revenue.

  • Market validation: Strong demand in both the U.S. and Scandinavia.

  • Operational capability: On-time manufacturing and fulfillment.

  • Customer satisfaction: Positive reviews and testimonials.

With those results in hand, you are in a much stronger position to approach distributors, retailers, and even potential investors for the next stage of growth.

A successful Kickstarter becomes not just a fundraising event, but a proof point that the business can execute internationally. That proof can be far more valuable than the funds themselves when you're negotiating distribution agreements.
