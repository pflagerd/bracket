Phase 1 – Prepare for Crowdfunding (Your Current Stage)

This should be your immediate focus.

  1. File a Provisional Patent Application so you have "Patent Pending" status before publicly revealing the design.

  2. Obtain manufacturing quotes from several factories (U.S. and overseas).

  3. Calculate your exact funding goal, including tooling, manufacturing, shipping, taxes, platform fees, and a contingency.

  4. Create your brand (company name, logo, website, packaging concept).

  5. Produce a professional campaign video and photography using your real prototypes.

Only after these are complete would you launch the crowdfunding campaign.

Phase 2 – Crowdfunding Campaign

This is where you raise the capital to pay for:

  • Production tooling

  • First manufacturing run

  • Packaging

  • Freight

  • Initial inventory

  • Marketing

  • Patent costs

  • Working capital

Given everything you've told me, I would not ask for the minimum amount needed to survive. I would ask for the amount needed to build a real company.

Since you are considering manufacturing in the United States while initially selling in Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Finland, and Iceland, your costs will be higher than manufacturing in Asia, but you'll gain advantages in quality control, faster design changes, and a compelling "Made in USA" story.

What does the $100,000 marketing budget cover?

It could include activities such as:

  • Digital advertising (Meta, Google, Pinterest, YouTube)

  • PR outreach to Scandinavian home improvement and design media

  • Product samples for influencers and reviewers

  • Professional photography and video for ongoing campaigns

  • Email marketing and CRM tools

  • Trade show participation (if appropriate)

  • Point-of-sale materials for retailers

  • Translation and localization into Swedish, Norwegian, Danish, and Finnish

  • Launch promotions and introductory offers

I would also separate marketing into two phases

Phase 1 – Pre-launch (before Kickstarter)

Budget: $20,000–40,000

Objective:

  • Build an email list.

  • Create awareness.

  • Validate demand.

  • Generate excitement before the campaign.

Phase 2 – Post-funding (after Kickstarter)

Budget: $60,000–100,000

Objective:

  • Drive direct sales.

  • Approach retailers and distributors.

  • Build long-term brand recognition.

  • Support repeat purchases and future product launches.

One thing I would change

Since your target market is Scandinavia, I would not spend the majority of the marketing budget trying to reach U.S. consumers unless you decide to launch on Kickstarter with a global audience.

Instead, I would focus on:

  • Sweden

  • Norway

  • Denmark

  • Finland

These four markets are culturally similar, geographically close, and easier to support with localized marketing and customer service. Once you've established a strong presence there, expanding into Germany, the Netherlands, the UK, and the rest of Europe becomes more straightforward.

My recommendation

If your ambition is to create a company that could eventually become a leading Scandinavian hardware and home organization brand, I would target a total funding amount in the range of $500,000–750,000 over the first 12–18 months. That level of funding gives you enough capital to:

  • Manufacture without cutting corners.

  • Build inventory.

  • Launch professionally.

  • Invest meaningfully in Scandinavian marketing.

  • Maintain sufficient working capital while revenue ramps up.

The biggest mistake many hardware startups make is raising just enough money to manufacture the product, leaving too little to market it effectively. A great product still needs sustained visibility to generate sales.

One final thought

From everything you've shared, I don't think you're building "just a shelf bracket." The larger opportunity is to build a premium Scandinavian storage and organization brand—a company known for innovative, beautifully designed mounting and storage solutions. If you succeed with the first product, it can become the foundation for an entire product family rather than a one-off invention.
